# Conflict-Intensity-and-Healthcare-Disruption
This research project examines the relationship between conflict intensity and the disruption of healthcare systems in Ukraine through a geospatial and temporal analysis of attacks on health facilities and related assets. The study integrates event data from the Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project, records from the Uppsala Conflict Data Program, and verified incidents of attacks on healthcare infrastructure.
The main findings show that attacks on healthcare assets rose sharply in the opening weeks of the full-scale invasion that began on 24 February 2022, coinciding with the period of highest weekly conflict event counts and fatalities recorded by the Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project. These healthcare attacks were geographically concentrated in the same oblasts that experienced the heaviest fighting, particularly Donetsk, Kharkiv, Luhansk, Kyiv, and Zaporizhia. Events from the Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project that explicitly mention hospitals, clinics, or ambulances also cluster within these high-intensity zones.
Overall, the results demonstrate that disruption of health systems is neither randomly distributed in space nor independent of the tempo of violence. Instead, attacks on healthcare infrastructure closely track both the timing and the geography of intensified organised conflict, as measured by the Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project and the Uppsala Conflict Data Program. This supports the central claim that conflict intensity is a primary driver of healthcare-system disruption during the early phase of the full-scale war in Ukraine.
